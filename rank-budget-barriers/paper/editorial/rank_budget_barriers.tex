\begin{abstract}
We study the information retained by local data in exact interpolation
algorithms. A matrix-valued Hermite transport identity connects
polynomial degree, nonuniform jet multiplicities, local factor widths
and the rank of a final input-value matrix. We give an exact
fixed-decoder precision criterion, a finite observation-lattice bound
and a source-specific optimum over retained dyadic jet profiles. On
genuine bounded-occurrence CNFs a common oriented matching controls both
the rational and tensor-contraction entry points of the studied
manuscript, including fixed affine mixtures with cancellation and shared
registers. An independent projection estimate gives the published
optimized iterator exponent even when only attainable measurement tuples
are enumerated. Conversely, certified layouts admit a linear-work
cross-method product on the same family, delimiting any extension beyond
the affine interface. The field, finite-algebra, CNF, common matching and literal root-visit theorems have Lean certificates. Source algorithms and external runtime upper bounds are cited explicitly. Classical precedents
and open model-coverage questions are explicit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and main
conclusions}\label{introduction-and-main-conclusions}

Interpolation algorithms replace one difficult object by local data and
recover its value by fixed linear operations. The central question here
is what those local data must retain when the final value is viewed as a
function of the original input. A single output number has no useful
rank lower bound. Its matrix of values over two independently varied
parts of the input can have large rank. Polynomial test vectors make
this distinction compatible with Hermite interpolation, higher
multiplicities and finite precision.

We develop one accounting framework around the identity

\[
 L_a(A)=\sum_t(D_t\otimes I)L_t(A)(E_t\otimes I).
 \tag{U.1}
\]

Here \(L_t(A)\) is multiplication by the actual matrix jet at node
\(t\), and \(D_t,E_t\) are explicit scalar interpolation maps. If the
entries of \(A(T)\) have degree at most \(d\), the total observation
multiplicity is \(S\), and the anchor is invertible, then each
\(b\le S-d\) supplies a rank-\(bN\) target in (U.1). Thus
\(bN\le\sum_t\operatorname{rank}L_t(A)\). Whole-jet factor widths
\(r_t\) give \(\sum_t k_t(N-r_t)\le Nd\). Pointwise matrix ranks do not
give this multiplicity-weighted inequality. We give explicit
counterexamples to that substitution and to a naive extension to
colliding residues.

The field inequality is a classical consequence of polynomial matrix
theory. Interpolation of products in local algebras also has a
substantial history, including the Chudnovsky--Chudnovsky method and
Randriambololona's interpolation systems. Our aim is not to rename those
results. We use their common transport identity to retain three pieces
of information that matter for algorithm barriers: the exact loss of
precision in the specified decoder, the permitted input dependence of
local representations, and the distinction between a representation and
an execution. The literature audit compares the precise hypotheses
rather than treating similarity of terminology as novelty.

For fraction fields of discrete valuation rings we determine, for a
fixed decoder and independent coefficient errors, the exact
coordinatewise accuracy needed to retain the anchor residue. This
remains valid when distinct field nodes collide modulo the uniformizer.
A complementary finite-module-length bound measures the actual image of
the observation lattice; it uses Smith form over the DVR only, never
over a nonprincipal double-truncation ring. Finite algebra readouts have
their own exact bilinear capacity. These facts allow Taylor statistics,
shared information and separated tensor products to be treated as
channels of the same final value matrix.

We apply the framework to two concrete entry points in OpenAI's October
25, 2026 manuscript: the direct bounded-occurrence rational clause
routine, and the sparse-CNF root contraction in Appendix D. We also
analyze supplied sparse polynomial systems. Four-copy implication cycles
put the same pinned expander CNFs into both entry points. A weighted
simultaneous-correlation argument selects one oriented induced matching
for their combined actual layouts. Thus the two routes are compared on
the same target, not on different worst inputs. At the published scales
a fixed affine mixture has a superpolynomial entrance requirement even
if it uses signed cancellation, completed Taylor registers, shared
caches, and bounded-capacity input-dependent transfers.

The second main outcome is a limit on this result. On
triangle-replacement expanders, admissible source layouts permit the
same count function to be computed by two full coordinate products and
one multiplication across coordinates. The products are computable in
linear indexed work. An independent refinement uses one MAIN coordinate
and one Appendix D coordinate. The functions used within the groups are
arbitrary permitted local functions; we do not identify them with
particular native numerical probes. This is a strict separation between
the affine representation interface and a natural nonlinear extension.
It prevents an assertion that rank alone excludes all hybrids built from
the two local mechanisms.

\section{Hermite transport, precision and matrix
degree}\label{hermite-transport-precision-and-matrix-degree}

\subsection{The reconstruction
inequality}\label{the-reconstruction-inequality}

Let \(K\) be a field and \(N\ge1\). Let \(A(T)\in K[T]^{N\times N}\)
have entry degree at most \(d\) and \(\det A(a)\ne0\) at an anchor
\(a\in K\). Select distinct \textbf{field} nodes \(t\in K\), positive
lengths \(k_t\), and nonzero coordinate scales \(\alpha_t,\beta\in K\).
Set \(S=\sum_t k_t\), \(1\le b\le S-d\), \(B_t=K[z]/z^{k_t}\), and
\(B_a=K[w]/w^b\). All coefficients mean ordinary monomial coefficients
after substitution, or Hasse coefficients, in arbitrary characteristic.
Denote by \(L_t(P)\) multiplication of a vector in \(B_t^N\) by
\(P\in B_t^{N\times N}\), written over \(K\) in coefficient-first order.
Write \(J_t=A(t+\alpha_tz)\bmod z^{k_t}=\sum_j A_{tj}z^j\). Write
\(L_a\) for multiplication by \(A(a+\beta w)\bmod w^b\).

Define two scalar matrices independent of \(A\).

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \(E_t:K^b\longrightarrow K^{k_t}\) sends the coefficient vector of
  \(h(w)\), \(\deg h<b\), to the jet of \(h((T-a)/\beta)\) at
  \(T=t+\alpha_tz\).
\item
  The inverse of the Hermite evaluation isomorphism
  \(K[T]_{<S}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_t B_t\) exists by CRT over \(K\).
  Apply the inverse to a vector supported at node \(t\), substitute
  \(T=a+\beta w\), and retain orders below \(b\). This defines
  \(D_t:K^{k_t}\longrightarrow K^b\).
\end{itemize}

Let \(Z_t\) multiply a scalar jet by \(z\), and set
\(G_{tj}=D_tZ_t^jE_t\). On vector coefficients, write scalar maps
tensored with \(I_N\).

\textbf{Theorem 2.1 (Hermite reconstruction with finite precision).} In
the stated field setting, \[
 L_a=\sum_t (D_t\otimes I_N)L_t(J_t)(E_t\otimes I_N)
     =\sum_{t,j} G_{tj}\otimes A_{tj} . \tag{H.1}
\] In particular, the left side has rank \(bN\). The same compression
witnesses this rank after independent finite-precision replacement,
under the following conditions. Let \(K\) be the fraction field of a DVR
\(\mathcal O\), with uniformizer \(\pi\) and normalized valuation \(v\).
Require all the true local jet coefficients and the anchor jet
coefficients through order \(b-1\) to be integral, and require
\(\det A(a)\) to be a unit. Allow
\(\widetilde A_{tj}\equiv A_{tj}\pmod{\pi^{P_{tj}}}\) independently,
entry by entry. For a target precision \(p\ge1\), the necessary and
sufficient condition for \textbf{all these errors}, for this fixed
compression, to change every block entry by an element of
\(\pi^p\mathcal O\) is \[
       P_{tj}+\min_{i,l} v((G_{tj})_{il})\ \ge p\quad\text{for every }t,j. \tag{H.2}
\] Here \(v(0)=+\infty\). Under (H.2), for every permissible choice of
local lifts \(\widetilde J_t=\sum_j\widetilde A_{tj}z^j\), \[
 bN\ \le\ \sum_t\operatorname{rank}_K L_t(\widetilde J_t),\qquad
 bN\ \le\ \sum_{t,j}\operatorname{rank}_K G_{tj}\ \operatorname{rank}_K\widetilde A_{tj}. \tag{H.3}
\] One may replace either local matrix by a sum of entrance matrices and
take the sum of their individual ranks. In the second inequality
identical scalar entrance matrices \(U_c\) reused across coefficients
combine to one \(H_c\otimes U_c\), with \(H_c=\sum_{tj}u_{tj,c}G_{tj}\)
for fixed coefficients \(u_{tj,c}\). This gives
\(bN\le\sum_c\operatorname{rank}H_c\operatorname{rank}U_c\). Counting a
reused entrance several times is unnecessary in this version. For a
rank-\(r\) residue anchor, restricting to an invertible \(r\)-minor
gives \(br\) on the left. The same restriction works for rectangular
matrices.

\emph{Proof.} A test column \(h(w)\) of degree below \(b\) represents
\(h((T-a)/\beta)\). The product \(A(T)h((T-a)/\beta)\) has degree at
most \(d+b-1<S\). Hence the exact scalar inverse CRT in each component
recovers the product from its local jets. Restriction at the anchor
gives (H.1). In coefficient-first order \(L_a\) is block lower
triangular with \(b\) copies of \(A(a)\) on its diagonal. Its
determinant is \(\det(A(a))^b\). The difference of the compressed
matrices is exactly
\(\sum_{t,j}G_{tj}\otimes(\widetilde A_{tj}-A_{tj})\). Condition (H.2)
bounds every entry by the nonarchimedean triangle inequality.
Conversely, choose one coefficient error, one matrix unit and an entry
attaining the minimum valuation to see necessity. The compressed lift is
integral and has the same invertible residue as \(L_a\). It therefore
has rank \(bN\) over \(K\). Subadditivity, rank monotonicity under
composition, and Kronecker rank multiplicativity give all the
assertions. No residue-level CRT or multivariate Smith form has been
used. The necessity assertion is for the prescribed coefficientwise
accuracy under independent errors. Other decoders, constrained errors,
and mere preservation of rank can have weaker requirements.

\textbf{Interpretation.} With \(b=1\), (H.1) is scalar Hermite
interpolation followed by the full-rank terminal matrix test. With
\(b=S-d\), every independent test column of degree below \(S-d\)
simultaneously passes through the same local maps. In exact arithmetic,
a local jet admitting a factorization through \(B_t^{r_t}\) has
\(\operatorname{rank}_K L_t(J_t)\le k_t r_t\). Consequently \[
                       \sum_t k_t(N-r_t)\le Nd. \tag{H.4}
\] If \(S\le d\), (H.4) follows trivially for \(0\le r_t\le N\). Over
\((\mathcal O/\pi^{P_t})[z]/z^{k_t}\), a width-\(r_t\) local
factorization lifts by lifting \textbf{the factors} and multiplying
modulo \(z^{k_t}\). Its coefficientwise error is admissible, and its
lifted multiplication map still factors through \(K^{k_t r_t}\). Thus
(H.3) gives \(\sum_t k_t r_t\ge bN\) at every certified \(b\). The same
proof charges a sum of local factors. The coefficient capacity
\(\operatorname{rank}G_{tj}\le\min(b,k_t-j)\) follows from the shift.
When the anchor lies outside the full node set and \(b\ge k_t\), this
rank equals \(k_t-j\). Indeed \(E_t\) is surjective by translating a
polynomial of degree below \(k_t\). The single-node interpolant is
\(\prod_{s\ne t}(T-s)^{k_s}\) times a polynomial of degree below
\(k_t\), so \(D_t\) is injective since that product is a unit in the
anchor jet and \(b\ge k_t\).

\subsection{Exact sharp local profile}\label{exact-sharp-local-profile}

For a regular square \(A\), let \(e_{t,1}\le\cdots\le e_{t,N}\) be the
valuations of its local Smith factors over \(K[T]_{(T-t)}\). The
invertible matrices in this normal form stay invertible after reduction
to \(B_t\), and units and the nonzero coordinate scale do not affect
rank. Hence \[
 \delta_t:=Nk_t-\operatorname{rank}_K L_t(J_t)
         =\sum_{i=1}^N\min(k_t,e_{t,i}). \tag{H.5}
\] It follows that \[
 \sum_t\delta_t\le\sum_t v_{T-t}(\det A)\le\deg\det A\le Nd. \tag{H.6}
\] The first inequality is equality exactly when no sampled partial
multiplicity exceeds its cap. The second is equality exactly when the
determinant has no uncaptured irreducible factors. The final equality
holds when the grade-\(d\) leading coefficient matrix is nonsingular.
These conditions apply to a nonzero determinant, with constants
included. For general normal rank \(r\), replace \(N\) in (H.5) by
\(r\), keep the \(r\) finite local factors, and replace the determinant
in (H.6) by the monic greatest common divisor \(\Delta_r\) of the
nonzero \(r\)-minors. Smith form makes
\(v_t(\Delta_r)=\sum_{i=1}^r e_{t,i}\), and a nonzero minor has degree
at most \(rd\). More generally every \(s\le r\) has
\(v_t(\Delta_s)=\sum_{i\le s}e_{t,i}\) and
\(\sum_t\sum_{i\le s}\min(k_t,e_{t,i})\le\deg\Delta_s\le sd\). This uses
determinantal divisors, equivalently the corresponding Fitting ideals.
It does not require the determinant of a singular square matrix.

Local factor width \(r_t\) is a stronger statistic than a rank bound on
\(A(t)\). Over the principal local ring \(B_t\), the minimal factor
width of a matrix with Smith exponents is the number of exponents
\(e_{t,i}<k_t\). The upper bound is diagonal factorization. For the
converse, a submodule of a module generated by \(r_t\) elements over
this principal ideal ring also has at most \(r_t\) generators, by
lifting that submodule to a submodule of \(K[z]^{r_t}\) under a
surjection and using the PID submodule theorem. Thus the image of a
factorization through \(B_t^{r_t}\) needs at most \(r_t\) generators.
Its Smith decomposition exhibits exactly one cyclic summand for each
surviving exponent, and reduction of the image modulo its own
maximal-ideal multiple counts these summands. In particular
\(k_t(N-r_t)\le\delta_t\). This result concerns inner rank over the jet
algebra. Vanishing of \((r_t+1)\)-minors \textbf{modulo one power} need
not imply it.

For distinct monic irreducibles \(f_t\) of degrees \(c_t\), the same
identity in the quotient \(K[T]/(f_t^{k_t})\) gives
\(\delta_t=c_t\sum_i\min(k_t,v_{f_t}d_i)\), where \(d_i\) are the
invariant polynomials. The coefficient budget is \(\sum_t c_tk_t\), and
(H.6) holds with \(c_t\) multiplying each valuation. In the scalar-node
case a direct CRT proof of the grade bound uses injectivity of
\(v\mapsto Av\bmod\prod_t(T-t)^{k_t}\) on vector polynomials of degree
below \(S-d\). This argument works without choosing an invertible
field-valued anchor, even over a small finite field where one might not
exist. Equations (H.5)--(H.6) retain all detectable multiplicities that
the grade-only argument can discard.

\subsection{Sharpness and failure
modes}\label{sharpness-and-failure-modes}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Let \(g=\prod_{t\in U}(T-t)^{k_t}\), \(d=\deg g\), and \(A=gI_N\).
  Include at least one additional non-root node. At root nodes
  \(r_t=0\), elsewhere \(r_t=N\). Every inequality (H.4)--(H.6) is
  equality, and the local sum in (H.3) for \(b=S-d\) is exactly \(bN\).
  An anchor outside the nodes is regular. The diagonal family
  \(\operatorname{diag}(g_1,\ldots,g_N)\) realizes individually
  specified nonnegative local orders by taking
  \(g_i=\prod_t(T-t)^{e_{t,i}}\). The grade is \(\max_i\sum_t e_{t,i}\),
  which must be accounted for in sharpness assertions.
\item
  Pointwise corank supplies one unit of degree per lost dimension,
  without a free multiplicity factor. Take \(A(T)=TI_N\) at zero with
  \(k=2\) and anchor 1. Its evaluated rank is zero, its determinant has
  valuation \(N\), and its jet is \(zI_N\) with multiplication rank
  \(N\), so the purported bound \(2N\le N\) fails. Even the \(2\times2\)
  matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(z,z)\) over \(K[z]/z^2\) has zero
  determinant but inner rank two, since its image needs two generators.
  With \(N=3,r=1,k=2\), the matrix \(TI_3\) has every two-by-two minor
  divisible by \(T^2\), while its determinant has valuation three, below
  the proposed four.
\item
  A globally singular matrix with a zero column may have positive
  deficiency at every node without any determinant degree available to
  spend. The normal-rank version is the proper correction. An anchor of
  residue rank zero can also hide a nonzero constant matrix, for
  instance \(\pi I_N\pmod\pi\).
\item
  The quotient ring may contain distinct noncomaximal nodes. Over
  \(\mathbf Z/8\), \(A(T)=T^2-1\) has four different roots \(1,3,5,7\)
  and takes a unit value at zero. The degree is two. A two-node example
  quantifies the missing precision. Over \(\mathbf Z_{(2)}\), let
  \(A(T)=T\), use anchor 1 and field nodes 0 and \(2^P\), each known to
  precision \(P\). Both samples admit the zero lift. The two-node
  interpolation coefficient has valuation \(-P\), giving the one-bit
  shortfall for a residue-valued target in (H.2).
\item
  One must use the degree of the matrix entries for compression and the
  exact determinant degree for (H.6). Uniform scalar recovery on all
  polynomials of degree \(d\) from jets with \(S\le d\) cannot determine
  evaluation at an off-grid anchor. The polynomial
  \(\prod_t(T-t)^{k_t}\) and zero give the same jets and different
  anchor values. A promise on a smaller polynomial family can change
  this necessity.
\item
  Over a DVR quotient \(\mathcal O/(\pi^p)\), the minimum
  factorization width is bounded above by the fraction-field rank of
  every integral lift. In fact it equals the minimum of those ranks.
  To see the first inequality, take an integral basis of the free
  column module of a lift and reduce the resulting factorization.
  For the converse, lift the two factors in a minimum-width
  factorization and multiply them. This is a statement about
  unrestricted inner rank over the DVR quotient, not a bound on
  additional source constraints. An entrywise canonical lift of a
  product can add rank because it need not equal the product of
  canonical lifts. For instance the product of \((1,2)^T\) and \((1,2)\)
  modulo three has the rank-one integral lift
  \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\2&4\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), while
  entrywise representatives in \(\{0,1,2\}\) give determinant minus
  three. A low-rank matrix over the residue field need not describe a
  low-rank jet over \(K[z]/z^k\) or a low-cost integral family.
  Conversely, arbitrary global decoding, nonlinear input-dependent
  gates, and intrinsic running time are outside (H.3).
\end{itemize}

\section{Finite algebras and finite observation
lattices}\label{finite-algebras-and-finite-observation-lattices}

Let \(K\) be a field and \(B\) a finite-dimensional commutative
\(K\)-algebra. A completed local statistic is allowed to be one element
of \(B\), computed jointly with all its coefficients. For a fixed linear
readout \(\lambda:B\to K\), define the multiplication pairing

\[H_\lambda(u,v)=\lambda(uv),\qquad \rho(\lambda)=\operatorname{rank}_K H_\lambda.\]

\textbf{Proposition (exact readout capacity).} The smallest integer
\(r\) for which there exist linear forms \(a_i,b_i:B\to K\) satisfying

\[\lambda(uv)=\sum_{i=1}^r a_i(u)b_i(v)\quad(u,v\in B)\]

is \(\rho(\lambda)\), and \(\rho(\lambda)\le\dim_K B\). In any basis the
equality expresses the coefficient matrix of the pairing as a sum of
\(r\) outer products. Matrix rank gives the lower bound and a rank
factorization gives the upper bound. This is the ordinary bilinear-rank
theorem, not a new lower bound for unrestricted computation.

Consequently, when \(u\) and \(v\) are arbitrary functions of opposite
sides of a declared input cut, their one joint readout has at most
\(\rho(\lambda)\) scalar separated terms on that cut. For the oriented
matching restriction with \(m\) pairs and cut excess \(\Delta\), its
value matrix has rank at most \(\rho(\lambda)2^{m-\Delta}\). This uses
the single common output matrix, even when different registers use
different cuts. Summing fixed readouts of the same register first may
lower the pairing rank; charge the combined readout.

For \(B=K[z]/z^k\), write \(\lambda_j=\lambda(z^j)\). The pairing matrix
is the truncated Hankel matrix \(H_{ab}=\lambda_{a+b}\) for \(a+b<k\),
and zero otherwise. If \(\lambda=0\), its rank is zero. Otherwise let
\(r\) be the largest index with \(\lambda_r\ne0\). Rows and columns
greater than \(r\) vanish; reversing the first \(r+1\) columns gives a
triangular block with diagonal \(\lambda_r\). Therefore
\(\rho(\lambda)=r+1\). For the top-coefficient functional this is \(k\),
so the dimension bound is sharp.

For the double truncation \(B=K[z,w]/(z^k,w^\ell)\), the rectangular
monomial basis has size \(k\ell\). Reading the coefficient of
\(z^{k-1}w^{\ell-1}\) gives the permutation pairing between
complementary monomials, of rank \(k\ell\). Reading the coefficient of
\(z^a w^b\) has rank \((a+1)(b+1)\). For a general linear combination of
coefficients use its actual pairing matrix; there is no need to assign
the full dimension if this matrix has lower rank. This is ordinary
finite-algebra duality, not a Smith normal form over a two-variable
truncated ring.

For \(r\) symbol groups with elements \(u_g\in B\), first form the
products on the two sides of a chosen union of symbols. The preceding
proposition applies directly, with no enumeration of Taylor coefficient
compositions. If a full \(r\)-way scalar product representation is
desired and the field contains enough distinct interpolation points (as
it does over \(\mathbb Q\)), lift each \(u_g\) to its ordinary
polynomial representative and interpolate their unreduced product. One
variable needs \(r(k-1)+1\) values, and a rectangular double truncation
needs \([r(k-1)+1][r(\ell-1)+1]\) values. Truncate and apply \(\lambda\)
only after the ordinary interpolation. Evaluation at a nonzero scalar is
not an algebra homomorphism from \(K[z]/z^k\) and is never used as one.

\subsection{The common accounting
principle}\label{the-common-accounting-principle}

An observation channel has an encoder \(E\), a local operation \(L\) and
a decoder \(D\). Exact reconstruction gives an identity
\(M=\sum_jD_jL_jE_j\). A full-rank target forces
\(\operatorname{rank}M\le\sum_j\operatorname{rank}L_j\). The Hermite
theorem supplies this identity from degree and actual jets. The readout
proposition supplies the local capacity when a statistic is a product in
a finite algebra. A value-matrix restriction supplies the target rank
for the genuine count function. These are parts of one calculation: the
target is the same linear map, and each representation must preserve its
tested input directions through its local channels.

When the local statistics are reused, group identical entrance matrices
before estimating rank. For a fixed scalar affine suffix this means one
coefficient per distinct register. With input-dependent coefficients,
the capacity of the collected coefficient is part of the budget.
Products of distinct completed registers create new nonlinear channels;
the counterexamples show why such a channel cannot be declared cheap in
rank merely because it is one arithmetic gate. All these statements
concern representations until an execution contract proves that paid
operations must deliver the charged values.

The basis expansion, group regrouping, dimension bound and direct-main
terminal corollary are kernel-checked in
\nolinkurl{FiniteAlgebraReadout.lean}. The exact Hankel rank, the
rectangular quotient-basis identification and the explicit source
extraction have the proofs above and in the source audit. They are not
claimed as separately verified Lean statements.

\subsection{A complementary length bound for globally integral
polynomials}\label{a-complementary-length-bound-for-globally-integral-polynomials}

The next result uses the module length of the actual finite-ring image.
It is intrinsic to the observations and also covers colliding residues.
Here require \(A\in\mathcal O[T]^{N\times N}\) and that its residue
polynomial matrix \(\bar A\) be regular. Nodes and nonzero scales lie in
\(\mathcal O\). These stronger global integrality assumptions are
separate from Theorem 2.1's diskwise assumptions. Write \(\ell\) for
module length over \(\mathcal O\), normalized by
\(\ell(\mathcal O/\pi)=1\), and assume \(d<S\). Write \(m=S-d\). The
scalar observation map \[
 V:\mathcal O[T]_{<S}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_t(\mathcal O/\pi^{P_t})^{k_t},\qquad
 g\longmapsto \big(g(t+\alpha_t z)\bmod (\pi^{P_t},z^{k_t})\big)_t
\] has image \(H\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^S\mathcal O/\pi^{q_i}\), with
\(0\le q_1\le\cdots\le q_S\), allowing zero-length factors. Let
\(\overline L_t\) multiply by the actual double-truncated local jet.
Then \[
             \sum_t\ell(\operatorname{im}\overline L_t)\ \ge\ N\sum_{i=1}^{m} q_i. \tag{A.10}
\] If the local jets factor through \(r_t\) double-truncated channels,
the left side is at most \(\sum_t r_t k_t P_t\).

\emph{Proof.} Multiplication by \(A\) maps \(\mathcal O[T]^N_{<m}\) into
\(\mathcal O[T]^N_{<S}\). Its reduction modulo \(\pi\) is injective
because \(\bar A\) is regular and this is multiplication before any
truncation of the polynomial degree. A full-column minor of this map is
therefore a unit, so its image \(U\) is a rank-\(mN\) direct summand.
Applying \(V\) componentwise gives an image inside the direct sum of the
local multiplication images. The quotient \(H^N/V^N(U)\) is generated by
at most \((S-m)N\) elements, using the complementary summand. Every
quotient \(Q\) of a finite-length module \(G\) that can be generated by
\(j\) elements has length at most the sum of the largest \(j\) Smith
exponents of \(G\). Indeed, each layer \(\pi^{s-1}Q/\pi^sQ\) has residue
dimension bounded both by \(j\) and by \(\dim(\pi^{s-1}G/\pi^sG)\).
Summing the layer dimensions proves the assertion. Removing up to
\((S-m)N\) largest exponents of \(H^N\) leaves \(N\sum_{i\le m}q_i\). A
local factorization has image a subquotient of
\(((\mathcal O/\pi^{P_t})[z]/z^{k_t})^{r_t}\), of length \(r_t k_tP_t\).

For equal precisions \(P\), let \(\sigma_1\le\cdots\le\sigma_S\) be the
Smith valuations of the integral, untruncated-in-\(\pi\) confluent
evaluation matrix. Then \(q_i=\max(0,P-\sigma_{S+1-i})\). The
determinant of the evaluation matrix has valuation \[
 \sum_t \binom{k_t}{2}v(\alpha_t)+\sum_{s<t}k_sk_t v(t-s).
\] The determinant formula follows by the Newton basis with each node
repeated \(k_t\) consecutive times. Within the block for \(t\), the
\(j\)-th basis polynomial has a factor \((T-t)^j\), so all coefficient
rows below order \(j\) in that jet vanish and its order-\(j\)
coefficient is \(\alpha_t^j\prod_{s<t}(t-s)^{k_s}\). All earlier node
blocks vanish too. Multiplying these diagonal entries proves the
formula. Only the ordinary DVR coefficient matrix needs Smith form.

If the \(t\) are pairwise distinct modulo \(\pi\) and the \(\alpha_t\)
are units, \(V\) is surjective by integral CRT. Its precision list
consists of \(P_t\) repeated \(k_t\) times. Thus the total local
deficiency \(\sum_t (Nk_tP_t-\ell(\operatorname{im}\overline L_t))\) is
bounded by \(N\) times the sum of the \(d\) largest entries of that
list. This bound is sharp for every such list and every \(d\le S\).
Select integers \(0\le l_t\le k_t\) accounting for those \(d\) entries.
For \(A=I_N\prod_t(T-t)^{l_t}\), at each node all other factors are
units, so its deficiency is exactly \(Nl_tP_t\). This family has regular
reduction. If a unit-valued anchor is additionally required, assume that
an available residue lies outside the selected roots and use that
residue. The unrestricted sharpness statement above does not assert this
extra condition over a finite residue field.

For globally integral matrices with unit-scale distinct-residue nodes
one can use the actual determinant degree \(D=\deg\det\bar A\). Let
\(e_{t,i}\) be the Smith exponents of the reduction \(\bar A\) at
\(\bar t\). If \(s_{t,h}\in\mathbf Z_{\ge0}\cup\{\infty\}\) are the DVR
Smith valuations of the \(Nk_t\)-dimensional integral coefficient
multiplication matrix, then \[
 \ell(\operatorname{coker}\overline L_t)=\sum_h\min(P_t,s_{t,h})
 \le P_t\sum_i\min(k_t,e_{t,i}).
\] In fact the number of positive or infinite \(s_{t,h}\) is the nullity
modulo \(\pi\), which is (H.5) for \(\bar A\). Summing one precision
layer at a time gives \[
 \sum_t\ell(\operatorname{coker}\overline L_t)\le
 \sum_{s\ge1}\min\left(D,N\sum_{t:P_t\ge s}k_t\right). \tag{A.11}
\] This gives \(PD\) for common precision \(P\), and refines the
grade-only distinct-residue bound. For an explicit double truncation,
\(A(T)=\pi+T\) at zero has
\(\operatorname{coker}\overline L\cong\mathcal O/(\pi^P,\pi^k)\), of
length \(\min(P,k)\), since the quotient sets \(z=-\pi\). A Smith form
directly over \((\mathcal O/\pi^2)[z]/z^2\) is unavailable in general.
The ideal \((\pi,z)\) is not principal since its quotient by its square
has residue dimension two. Thus the single-row matrix \([\pi\ z]\) need
not reduce to a one-entry diagonal by invertible row and column
operations. Applying DVR Smith form to its ordinary coefficient map
avoids that error.

The coefficientwise precision certificate (H.2) is a linear
image-of-lattices computation. Caruso, Roe and Vaccon develop a general
theory of ultrametric lattice precision and apply it to matrix
computations. They precede the precision principle used here. The
specialized degree-constrained reconstruction, the local representation
capacities and the source application carry the interpretive content.

precision} ·

\subsection{Rank cost of one joint
statistic}\label{rank-cost-of-one-joint-statistic}

For any fixed functional \(\Lambda:B\to\mathbb Q\), an \(r\)-way product
of independently supplied group elements in (M.2) has scalar full-group
product length at most

\[
 P_r(J,\ell)=(r(J-1)+1)(r(\ell-1)+1),
\tag{M.3}
\]

where \(\ell=1\) covers direct counts. Lift each group element to its
unique polynomial of individual degrees below \(J,\ell\). Their
unreduced product has the two degrees at most \(r(J-1)\) and
\(r(\ell-1)\). Interpolate the ordinary product on a rectangular grid of
the two indicated numbers of distinct rational values. Applying
\(\Lambda\) after truncation to the interpolating basis produces fixed
scalars. At a grid point the product of the evaluations is already one
full-group scalar product. Evaluation itself need not preserve
multiplication in the \emph{quotient}. This calculation proves (M.3)
without making that false assumption. For the single chosen cut, at most
\(J\ell\) separated terms suffice. Form the products within each side of
the cut in \(B\). The final functional of their product is a bilinear
form on \(B\times B\) of matrix rank at most
\(\dim_{\mathbb Q}B=J\ell\). Rank factorization gives the claim. Both
bounds permit simultaneous computation of all retained orders within a
group.

More generally let an entrance \(a\) at node \(t(a)\) receive two
group-local vectors \(u_a,v_a\) on opposite sides of \(S_{t(a)}\),
contributing \(u_a^\top M_a v_a\) to a fixed linear output network. Its
weight is \(w_a=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}M_a\). The effective
matrix incorporates fixed postprocessing, including the joint use of the
same entrance by several output orders. When several fixed bilinear
coefficient matrices act on the same two inputs, combine their terminal
coefficients first and take the rank of the resulting matrix. A
different representation may use smaller orders, different statistics at
every node, distinct side dimensions, or even an arbitrary tabulated
interface of known bipartite rank. The terminal budget is
\(\sum_a w_a\). In particular, local data compression is measured by the
dimension or rank it actually transmits. A node's output may depend on
all pins through charged bilinear entrances.

\subsection{The terminal theorem and high Taylor
orders}\label{the-terminal-theorem-and-high-taylor-orders}

For every structural schedule as above, every fixed sum of full-group
scalar products, using at each product any one of the \(Q\) actual node
partitions, whose value is integral for each sign word and agrees in
parity with the final count has at least

\[
   2^{cN/\sqrt Q}
\tag{M.4}
\]

uniform bound \(C\). The stronger interface satisfies
\(\sum_a w_a\ge2^{cN/\sqrt Q}\). This is a lower bound on the true exact

\subsection{Exact counts with many values on an entirely satisfiable

\subsection{A weighted common
matching}\label{a-weighted-common-matching}

\textbf{Theorem 5.1 (Weighted common matching).} Let finitely many graph-only layouts \(s\) on \(V\)
have \(k_s\ge1\) coordinates and pin-label laws dominated by \(C_s\)
times product-uniform measure. At each coordinate fix one
\(\eta\)-balanced symbol union \(P_{si}\), with \(0<\eta\le1/2\). Give
every event in layout \(s\) weight \(w_s>0\), and put

\[
 H=\sum_s w_s\sqrt{C_s k_s}.
\]

There are constants \(a_{\eta,\gamma}>0\) and \(B_{\eta,\gamma}<\infty\)
that apply to all cubic input graphs under these assumptions. If

\[
 \frac{B_{\eta,\gamma}H^2}{N}\sum_s k_s/w_s^2<1 ,
 \tag{X.2}
\]

there is one induced matching of \(m\) edges and one row/column
orientation \(A\mid B\) of its endpoints such that

\[
  \left|\ |A\cap P_{si}|-|B\cap P_{si}|\ \right|
       \ge a_{\eta,\gamma}\frac{Nw_s}{H}
       \qquad\text{for all }s,i.                         \tag{X.3}
\]

The matching and partition are selected for the union of the layouts
before any entrance, computation, or transcript is examined.

\emph{Proof.} On the uniform vertex probability space set
\(f_{si}=1_{P_{si}}-\Pr(P_{si})\). For every sign vector \(\epsilon\)
let \(Y=\sum_{si}\epsilon_{si}w_s f_{si}\). In one layout, the variance
of its signed sum is at most \(C_sk_s w_s^2\). Indeed, the actual
variance minimizes the quadratic error over all centering constants.
Center instead at the product-uniform mean and use density domination.
Under the product law different coordinates are independent and a signed
indicator has variance at most one. The triangle inequality in
\(L^2(V)\) gives \(\|Y\|_2\le H\), with no assumption on joint labels
from different layouts.

Choose \(\epsilon\) maximizing \(\mathbb E\rho(Y/H)\) for
\(\rho(t)=\sqrt{1+t^2}\), and set \(b=\rho'(Y/H)\). Flipping a sign
replaces \(Y/H\) by \(Y/H-h\), \(h=2\epsilon_{si}w_sf_{si}/H\). Here
\(w_s/H\le1\). The maximum and Taylor's formula give

\[
 \mathbb E(hb)\ge\tfrac12\mathbb E\!\left[
 h^2\inf_{|v-Y/H|\le2}(1+v^2)^{-3/2}\right].
\]

On \(\{|Y/H|\le2\}\), an event of probability at least \(3/4\), the
infimum is at least \(17^{-3/2}\) and \(|f_{si}|\ge\eta\). Consequently

\[
 |\mathbb E b f_{si}|\ge \frac{3\eta^2}{4\,17^{3/2}}\frac{w_s}{H}.
 \tag{X.4}
\]

Give the vertices divergence \(g=(\gamma/2)(b-\mathbb E b)\), after
decreasing \(\gamma\) to at most one if necessary. It has zero sum and
\(|g|\le\gamma\). Expansion bounds the absolute demand on every vertex
cut by that cut's edge capacity. Max-flow/min-cut provides an oriented
edge flow \(F_e\in[-1,1]\) with this divergence. For each prescribed cut
its signed flow therefore has magnitude at least a constant times
\(Nw_s/H\).

For completeness, independently mark each graph edge with probability
\(1/2\) and retain a marked edge only when all other edges sharing or
adjacent to an endpoint are unmarked. The retained edges form an induced
matching. Their individual retention probabilities \(\pi_e\) have a
fixed positive lower bound \(\pi_*\) by bounded degree. Give each
retained edge an independent orientation sign with mean
\(\pi_*F_e/\pi_e\) relative to a provisional orientation. The resulting
signed crossing count for a fixed cut has expectation
\(\pi_*\sum_{v\in P_{si}}g(v)\). Its variance is \(O(N)\), since each
bounded summand is independent of summands beyond fixed graph distance.
The probability of losing at least half the magnitude of its expectation
is at most \(B_{\eta,\gamma}H^2/(Nw_s^2)\) by Chebyshev. Summing over
the \(k_s\) cuts of each layout proves (X.3) under (X.2). The
bounded-majorant sign argument and the flow/thinning method have
precedents in Nazarov and the preceding study. The weights retain the
different route scales on one physical restriction.
\href{https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/aa774}{Nazarov, 1997} ·
\href{https://github.com/dch-and-Elara/some-research-inspired-by-openais-eth-preprint/tree/main/root-separation}{Prior unweighted
matching proof}

In particular take \(w_M=1/\sqrt Q\) and \(w_D=1/\sqrt u\). Then
\(H=\sqrt{C_M}+\sqrt{C_D}\) is bounded and (X.2) holds for large \(N\)
if \(Q^2+u^2=o(N)\). This includes the published schedules. Write
\(d_s\) for the minimum integer absolute excess for its layout on the
selected matching. Simultaneously

\[
 d_M=\Omega_\gamma(N/\sqrt Q),\qquad d_D=\Omega_\gamma(N/\sqrt u).
 \tag{X.5}
\]

More generally positive weights \(w_s=\alpha_s/\sqrt{C_s k_s}\),
\(\sum_s\alpha_s=1\), give margins proportional to
\(\alpha_sN/\sqrt{C_s k_s}\) whenever (X.2) holds. A different
\(\alpha\) may select a different witness.

\subsection{Rank on the fixed
restriction}\label{rank-on-the-fixed-restriction}

Fix all signs outside the matching to one. On \(A\mid B\), both sides
have \(m\) free bits and \(C_G\) has matrix \(D_\lor^{\otimes m}\) with
\(D_\lor=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
This matrix is unimodular. Work in the fraction field \(K\) of the
source integral domain \(\mathcal O\) with its characteristic-two
residue field. A final function \(F\) that is integral at every input
and has the correct residue has rank \(D_*=2^m\), by reducing the
determinant on this restriction. Products and summands may lie in \(K\)
and can have dyadic denominators. Denominator loss is checked at
reconstruction where needed. Write \(r(f)\) for the ordinary
evaluation-matrix rank of \(f\) on this one restriction and orientation.

An arbitrary scalar product \(U(\sigma_P)V(\sigma_{\bar P})\) across any
listed cut has \(r\le2^{m-d_s}\). If \(P\) contains \(a\) row and \(b\)
column bits of the matching, its matrix is, up to permutations, a
Kronecker product of matrices of shape \(2^a\times2^b\) and
\(2^{m-a}\times2^{m-b}\). Their ranks multiply and
\(\min(a,b)+\min(m-a,m-b)=m-|a-b|\). The same bound holds when any
additional calculations are confined to their respective sides. A
two-view term with overlapping input sets has the corresponding bound
\(2^{m-(|a-b|-\ell)_+}\), where \(\ell\) counts row bits from the excess
side read on the opposite view plus column bits from the other side read
on the excess view. Condition on those bits together with the two
minority sets. Each resulting piece separates across \(A\mid B\). For a
product/sum factor graph with latent indices, conditioning on an actual
variable separator supplies the product of separator-domain sizes as a
rank upper bound.
\href{https://github.com/dch-and-Elara/some-research-inspired-by-openais-eth-preprint/tree/main/root-separation}{Overlap proof} ·
\href{https://github.com/dch-and-Elara/some-research-inspired-by-openais-eth-preprint/tree/main/root-separation}{Latent and visible
separators}

For MAIN a native tuple can be a product in \(\mathcal A=K[z]/(z^J)\).
Group its factors on the two sides as \(u,v\in\mathcal A\), and apply a
fixed \(K\)-linear readout \(\lambda\). The coefficient matrix of the
bilinear form \(\lambda(uv)\) is \(B_{ab}=\lambda(z^{a+b})\) for
\(a+b<J\), and zero otherwise. For \(\lambda\ne0\), put
\(k=\max\{j:\lambda(z^j)\ne0\}\). Rows and columns past \(k\) vanish.
The top \((k+1)\)-square matrix becomes triangular with nonzero diagonal
after its columns are reversed. Thus its scalar bilinear rank is
precisely

\[
 s_\lambda=k+1\le J,\qquad r(\lambda(uv))\le s_\lambda\,2^{m-d_M}.
 \tag{X.6}
\]

For zero readout use \(s_\lambda=0\). An arbitrary changed basis of the
coefficient vector preserves this bilinear rank. Alternatively,
interpolating the ordinary product of the \(r_M\) degree-\(<J\)
representatives at \(r_M(J-1)+1\) distinct rationals yields that many
full scalar coordinate-group products after applying the readout. These
two upper bounds address different choices of entrance. Neither requires
enumerating all weak compositions of a coefficient order. D's root
scalar product of independently lifted local residues has scale one in
\(K\). A global nonlinear residue section remains subject to an
independent rank certificate.

\subsection{A frontier bound with shared
registers}\label{a-frontier-bound-with-shared-registers}

Fix a routed leaf or a single straight-line program. A \emph{frontier}
is a set of distinct cached input values \(P_j\), such as completed
MAIN/D local products. After replacing each of their uses by the same
scalar indeterminate \(z_j\), suppose the remaining expression is
pointwise affine in these indeterminates over the algebra of functions
of the original input:

\[
       H_0(\sigma)+\sum_{j}H_j(\sigma)z_j .                  \tag{X.7}
\]

The functions \(H_j\) have to be bounded on the same restriction. Any
raw-input route bypassing the frontier is included in \(H_0\) or in
these coefficient functions. One sufficient circuit syntax allows
addition, fixed scalar maps, and multiplication by cached coefficient
functions independent of the \emph{formal} indeterminates. The
coefficient functions may share raw inputs with the \(P_j\). A transfer
\(H_j\) is the coefficient after collecting all uses of \(z_j\),
including all signs and cancellations. For a fixed scalar linear DAG the
\(H_j\) are constants, regardless of the number of paths or fan-out. A
fixed linear readout from a jet register first gives a single
\(\lambda_j\) per distinct register. Equation (X.6) then applies. For
several input-dependent readouts, include their scalar coordinate
components or certify their combined rank separately.

\textbf{Theorem 5.2 (Shared-register frontier bound).} Let \(F\) be integral with the residue in (X.1) and
let \(G_\ell\in\{0,1\}\) be the indicators of a finite partition of the
inputs into reached transcripts. On transcript \(\ell\) let (X.7)
describe its output after substituting \(z_j=P_{\ell j}(\sigma)\). Use
arbitrary total extensions off that transcript, multiplying each
contribution by \(G_\ell\). Then

\[
\begin{split}
 D_*&\le\sum_\ell r(G_\ell H_{\ell0})
       +\sum_{\ell,j} r(G_\ell H_{\ell j} P_{\ell j})\\
 &\le\sum_\ell r(G_\ell H_{\ell0})
       +\sum_{\ell,j}\min\{D_*,
                   r(G_\ell H_{\ell j})\,r(P_{\ell j})\}.
\end{split}
\tag{X.8}
\]

Any tighter bound on the augmented term in the first line can be
substituted, including a term-relative guarded scope or a latent
separator. If the same \(P_j\) is reused across transcripts, first
collect \(\sum_\ell G_\ell H_{\ell j}\) and count \(P_j\) once wherever
that gives a better bound.

\emph{Proof.} One guard is true on each assignment. The entrywise sum of
the guarded terms therefore equals the restricted \(F\) over \(K\). Rank
is subadditive under addition. For functions \(a,b\), their product
gives the Hadamard product of their matrices, with
\(r(ab)\le r(a)r(b)\). Expand each input as a sum of rank-one outer
products, then multiply them pairwise. All matrices have rank at most
\(D_*\). These facts prove both lines. A local Boolean test on each of a
term's two views folds directly into its respective factor, including
negated tests for false branches. A deterministic two-party transcript
relative to \(A\mid B\) is a rectangle because after its prefix is fixed
every test belongs to the specified party. Its indicator has rank at
most one. An arbitrary Boolean test of both sides can have full rank.
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.4006}{Arvind, Joglekar and Srinivasan,
Prop. 1} · \href{https://doi.org/10.1145/800070.802208}{Mehlhorn and
Schmidt, 1982}

An arbitrary fixed arithmetic DAG also admits a \emph{certificate
calculation}. Give constants rank budget one, an input gate its
certified bound, and scalar sums and entrywise products the rules

\[
 R(g+h)\le\min(D_*,R(g)+R(h)),\qquad
 R(gh)\le\min(D_*,R(g)R(h)).
 \tag{X.9}
\]

Here the right side is an admissible upper budget, and any stronger
semantic certificate can replace it. Apply the rules in topological
order, evaluating one budget per gate. An equivalent distinct-node
linear frontier can be obtained by stopping a reverse traversal of
fixed-scalar linear gates at the first nonlinear result or primitive
reached along each reverse path, then collecting repeated node
identifiers. A new nonlinear gate may enter the frontier with its
multiplicative bound. A single such gate can already reach full rank.
Proofs that a larger subcircuit stays within a declared scope or has an
affine suffix can give a smaller bound than the literal recurrence.
Equation (X.9) is a bound on the output matrix of a circuit of the
specified kind. A sum over its expanded arithmetic terms has no
automatic correspondence to an execution trace.

\subsection{Mixed resource tradeoffs}\label{mixed-resource-tradeoffs}

For a single fixed straight-line affine suffix, with constant downstream
coefficients, zero bypass, \(h_M\) distinct MAIN jet values, and \(h_D\)
distinct D scalar entrances, (X.5)--(X.8) imply

\[
        1\le \sum_{j\in M}s_{\lambda_j}\,2^{-d_M}
                        +h_D\,2^{-d_D}
          \le J h_M\,2^{-d_M}+h_D\,2^{-d_D}.               \tag{X.10}
\]

For multiple transcripts, (X.10) requires the same capacity bound for
every guarded entrance and counts those entrances across the leaves.
Constants inside a leaf alone do not control its guard. General routing
must use (X.8).

Both sets of terms may have arbitrary signs and may cancel. It follows
that \(J h_M\ge2^{d_M-1}\) or \(h_D\ge2^{d_D-1}\). At the published
parameters this gives at least \(2^{\Omega(N/\sqrt{\log N})}\) distinct
scalar/jet entrances up to a polynomial factor. If the D capacity is
\(o(2^{d_D})\), (X.10) gives \((1-o(1))2^{d_M}/J\) MAIN entrances. This
last bound exposes MAIN's own \(Q\) in place of \(u\). Analogous
conclusions hold when cumulative overlap, bypass, or nonconstant
transfer costs are included explicitly via (X.8).

The common exact-count construction in the source application has
terminal \(2^{1+\sum_{v\in T}\sigma_v}+C_G(\sigma)\) with
\(|T|=\lfloor N/4\rfloor\). It yields at least \(|T|+1\) distinct
positive counts and the same terminal residue. Its MAIN incidence
density is bounded by an absolute constant, and the published D cell
estimate gives density at most 40 eventually, hence symbol mass at most
\(5/8\). The same weighted matching proof and (X.10) apply on that
single always-satisfiable family. Only the counting task has this
obstruction there. \href{https://github.com/dch-and-Elara/some-research-inspired-by-openais-eth-preprint/tree/main/rank-budget-barriers}{Gadget and
exact source counts}

An actual mixed example uses a fixed sign \(\sigma_v\) to run direct
MAIN when the sign is one and D when it is zero, returning the
corresponding answer. Multiply the MAIN terms by \(\sigma_v\) and the D
terms by \(1-\sigma_v\). In each term the guard can be assigned to the
group containing that sign, preserving its rank bound. The
constant-depth switch is a two-transcript instance of (X.8). No claim
about the general SAT-to-parity preprocessing is needed.

For a variable-length parser, put \(a_\ell\) equal to the contribution
of leaf \(\ell\) in a valid upper budget in (X.8), divided by \(D_*\).
Discard unreachable leaves on the restricted domain. If the public
branch transcripts have prefix-free lengths \(b_\ell\), then

\[
     \exists\hbox{ a reached }\ell:\quad 2^{b_\ell}a_\ell\ge1. \tag{X.11}
\]

Indeed \(\sum_\ell a_\ell\ge1\) whereas Kraft's inequality gives
\(\sum_\ell2^{-b_\ell}\le1\). Otherwise the first sum would be strictly
smaller than the second. The same realized input therefore witnesses the
\nolinkurl{transcript/entrance} tradeoff. For example, suppose there is
zero bypass, all augmented entrances have rank at most
\(\rho D_*2^{-d}\), and on every reached leaf the implementation
materializes its \(h_\ell\) distinct required values at a rate of at
most \(W\) values per unit charged work \(T_\ell\). Then on one reached
leaf

\[
       2^d\le 2^{b_\ell}\rho h_\ell
                          \le 2^{b_\ell}\rho W T_\ell .       \tag{X.12}
\]

With uniform \(b_\ell\le b\) this gives worst-case work at least
\(2^{d-b}/(\rho W)\). For a fixed schedule use \(b=0\). With \(b=o(d)\)
and polynomial \(\rho,W\), the exponential scale persists. If \(b\) can
be of order \(d\), the tradeoff permits an ordinary linear scan. The
variable batch version counts \(\sum_e W_ec_e\) over actual paid events
of cost \(c_e\), with \(W_e\) a per-unit delivery rate and requires
coverage by their returned values. Distinct outputs of a batch or cached

\section{Attainable measurements and the optimized iterator
cost}\label{attainable-measurements-and-the-optimized-iterator-cost}

The exact set of Boolean measurements already has exponentially many
elements when the exceptional spectral window is too short. This gives
the source's optimized iterator exponent even after discarding
impossible measurement tuples and removing linearly redundant
coordinates. The statement concerns the numerical measurements and
enumerations specified below. An algorithm computing the same final
count through a different statistic has a separate cost analysis.

\subsection{A finite lower bound for the Boolean measurement
image}\label{a-finite-lower-bound-for-the-boolean-measurement-image}

There are \(n\) assignment variables and \(M\) occupied incidences, with
every variable occurring. Let \(r\ge2\), \(Q\ge2\), \(1\le R<Q\),
\(D=r^{2Q}\). A full cell is \(u=(a,d)\in[r]^Q\times[r]^Q\). Every
incidence of a fixed variable \(v\) has the same first word \(a_v\).
Number the copies so that each occupied pair (copy, cell) contains
exactly one incidence. Let \(c_u\) count all incidences in cell \(u\),
with \(c_u\le 2M/D\). The placement map \(\iota\) writes \(x_v\) at its
occupied positions and zero elsewhere. For any interval
\(\mathcal I\subseteq\{0,\ldots,Q\}\) of \(R\) integers, let
\(C^{\mathcal B}\) consist of the source's tensor-product swap
eigenvectors whose eigenweight is outside \(\mathcal I\). Write
\(\widetilde C\) for its direct sum across copies and
\(\widehat C=\widetilde C\iota\). At a node the measured tuple is

\[
 Y_t(x)=(\widehat C|_{S_{t,g}}x_{S_{t,g}})_{g=1}^r,\qquad
 \mathcal Y_t^*=\{Y_t(x):x\in\{0,1\}^n\}.
\]

These are the source's numerical objects. The incidence placement sends
recurrences of one full cell to different copies. The local measurement
matrices retain the corresponding columns of the same global matrix.
\href{https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/main_full_version.tex\#L2240-L2276}{Exceptional
eigenbasis} ·
\href{https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/main_full_version.tex\#L2532-L2598}{Placement
and actual measurements}

\textbf{Proposition.} Put \(k(u)=|\{i:a(i)\ne d(i)\}|\) and
\(b_v=\#\{\text{incidences of }v\}\). For every node,

\[
 \log_2|\mathcal Y_t^*|\ \ge\ \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\widehat C
 \ \ge\
 \sum_{v=1}^n\frac{1}{b_v}
 \sum_{\text{incidences of }v\text{ at }u}
 \Pr\bigl[\operatorname{Bin}(k(u),1/2)\notin\mathcal I\bigr].
 \tag{F.A1}
\]

To prove this, take the real orthogonal projection \(P\) onto the row
space of \(\widetilde C\). Within a copy, two cells belong to the same
orbit of the coordinate swaps exactly when their unordered pairs agree
at every coordinate. Each tensor eigenvector is supported on a single
such orbit. An orbit with \(k\) unequal pairs has \(2^k\) positions. Its
normalized eigenvectors have squared entry \(2^{-k}\) at each position.
There are \(\binom{k}{j}\) of eigenweight \(j\), because any \(j\) of
the unequal coordinates may be assigned their antisymmetric factor. Thus
\(P\) has zero entries between different copy-orbit pairs and has
diagonal entry \(\Pr[\operatorname{Bin}(k(u),1/2)\notin\mathcal I]\) at
an occupied slot of cell \(u\).

The normalized columns \(e'_v=\iota e_v/\sqrt{b_v}\) are orthonormal
because their supports are disjoint. Within one copy, two positions in
the support of a fixed column cannot belong to the same orbit. The fixed
first word \(a_v\) and the unordered pair in each coordinate determine
the entire second word. Thus any same-orbit repetition would be the same
cell in that copy, which the placement excludes. Repeated cells in
different copies are orthogonal. It follows that \(\|Pe'_v\|^2\) is
exactly the \(v\)-summand on the right of (F.A1).

Let \(y_1,\ldots,y_m\) be an orthonormal basis of the span of the
\(Pe'_v\). As \(Py_j=y_j\), Bessel's inequality gives

\[
  \sum_v\|Pe'_v\|^2=\sum_{j=1}^m\sum_v|\langle e'_v,y_j\rangle|^2\le m.
\]

The maps \(P\iota\) and \(\widetilde C\iota\) have the same kernel.
Their common rank is \(m\), and the rational matrix \(\widehat C\) has
the same real and rational ranks. Select \(m\) independent columns of
\(\widehat C\) and vary only their Boolean inputs. The resulting \(2^m\)
global measurement vectors are distinct. Summing the group components of
\(Y_t(x)\) gives \(\widehat Cx\), so the same assignments give distinct
node tuples. This proves both inequalities.

\subsection{Uniform lower bounds on the diagonal
entries}\label{uniform-lower-bounds-on-the-diagonal-entries}

For a uniformly random full cell, \(k\) is binomial with parameters
\(Q,1-1/r\). With \(\xi=2^{1/4}(3/4)<1\), Markov's inequality gives

\[
 \Pr[k<Q/4]\le 2^{Q/4}\mathbb E[2^{-k}]\le \xi^Q.
\]

The cell bound gives at most \(2M\xi^Q\) occupied incidences in such
cells. If \(2M\xi^Q\le n/2\), at least \(n/2\) variables have all their
occurrences in cells with \(k\ge Q/4\). This holds for \(Q\) above a
constant on the cubic cloned input, where \(b_v=3\) and \(M=3n\). For
\(M\le n(\log_2 n)^B\), it holds once \(Q\ge C_B\log_2\log_2 n\), with a
constant depending only on \(B\). This last argument permits variable
degrees to vary.

For \(Q\) sufficiently large, \(R\le Q/16\), and \(Q/4\le k\le Q\),
absolute constants \(c,C>0\) give

\[
 \Pr[\operatorname{Bin}(k,1/2)\notin\mathcal I]\ \ge\
 c\exp(-C R^2/Q).                                      \tag{F.A2}
\]

Indeed, the \(R+\lfloor\sqrt k\rfloor\) consecutive integers starting at
\(\lfloor k/2\rfloor\) include at least \(\lfloor\sqrt k\rfloor\)
outside \(\mathcal I\), regardless of where \(\mathcal I\) lies. As
\(R\le k/4\), these integers lie in \([k/4,7k/8]\) for large \(k\). For
each of them the two-sided Stirling estimates give point probability at
least

\[
 c_1 k^{-1/2}\exp[-k D_{\mathrm{KL}}(j/k\|1/2)]
 \ \ge\ c_1 k^{-1/2}\exp[-C_1(R+\sqrt k+1)^2/k].
\]

In the final inequality, the relative entropy and its first derivative
vanish at \(1/2\), and its second derivative \(1/[x(1-x)]\) is bounded
on this compact interval. Summing the selected point probabilities
cancels the factor \(k^{-1/2}\). The inequalities \(k\ge Q/4\) and
\((R+\sqrt{k}+1)^2/k\le C_2(1+R^2/k)\) complete (F.A2). The same
reasoning works for an arbitrary excluded complement of a set of \(R\)
retained weights. \href{https://www.jstor.org/stable/2308012}{Herbert
Robbins, \emph{A Remark on Stirling's Formula}, 1955}

Combining (F.A1) and (F.A2), whenever \(2M\xi^Q\le n/2\) and
\(R\le Q/16\),

\[
  \log_2|\mathcal Y_t^*|\ge c_3 n\exp(-C_3 R^2/Q).       \tag{F.A3}
\]

\subsection{Optimization over
parameters}\label{optimization-over-parameters}

\textbf{Theorem.} Fix \(B\ge0\) and \(\kappa>0\). Suppose
\(n\le M\le n(\log_2 n)^B\) and the placement and cell guarantee above
hold. A constant factor in the upper bound for \(M\) can be absorbed by
enlarging \(B\). Consider an evaluator that pays at least one unit for
each of the following sets of iterations on a common routine call.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  It visits each Taylor pair \(U\subseteq E\), \(|E|\le q\), with
  \(q\ge\lfloor\kappa n R/Q\rfloor\).
\item
  At the empty Taylor pair it distinguishes and visits each value of the
  actual node summary \(Y_t\), at one or more nodes. It can enumerate
  the exact image and use any equivalent encoding.
\item
  For one node it explicitly visits every assignment of a largest
  variable group. It may do this across different attainable tuples.
  Each local assignment at the empty pair extends to a global
  assignment, so separate enumeration of every compatible fiber
  satisfies this condition.
\end{enumerate}

For every integer choice \(r\ge2\), \(Q>R\ge1\), every permitted
interval, and all sufficiently large \(n\), the total cost \(T\)
satisfies

\[
  \log_2 T\ \ge\ c_{B,\kappa}\,n\,\frac{(\log_2\log_2 n)^2}{\sqrt{\log_2 n}}.
  \tag{F.A4}
\]

The proof uses the maximum of the three iteration counts. They may
overlap in the same operation. Write \(L=\log_2 n\), \(l=\log_2 L\),
\(\alpha=l^2/\sqrt L\). If \(r\le1/\alpha\), a largest group has at
least \(n/r\ge n\alpha\) variables. Otherwise
\(\log_2 r\ge l/2-2\log_2 l=\Omega(l)\). At least one full cell is
occupied, so \(1\le 2M/D\) and \(D\le2M\). Consequently
\(Q\log_2 r\le\log_2(2M)/2=O(L)\), giving \(Q=O(L/l)\). This implication
uses the actual cell load and permits layouts beyond the source's
sufficient check \(2(b+w)D^2\le M\).

When \(Q\le C_B l\), \(q\ge\lfloor\kappa n/Q\rfloor\) and the Taylor
enumeration includes at least \(2^{\min(q,n)}\) choices. Its log cost is
\(\Omega(n/l)\), which exceeds \(n\alpha\) for large \(n\). This also
covers bounded \(Q\) and integer rounding. When \(R>Q/16\), the same
argument gives log cost \(\Omega_\kappa(n)\). We may therefore assume
the hypotheses of (F.A3). If \(\log_2T\ge n\alpha\), the result is
immediate. Otherwise (F.A3) implies

\[
   C_3 R^2/Q \ \ge\ \log(c_3/\alpha)=\Omega(l),
   \qquad R/Q\ \ge\ c_4 \sqrt{l/Q}\ \ge\ c_5 l/\sqrt L .
\]

Choose \(j=\lfloor c_6 n l/\sqrt L\rfloor\) with \(c_6>0\) small enough,
independently of the tuning. Then \(1\le j\le\min(q,n/2)\) for all
sufficiently large \(n\), and \(\log_2(n/j)=\Omega(l)\). The Taylor list
contains at least \(\binom{n}{j}\ge (n/j)^j\) pairs, whence (F.A4). The
published parameters, together with the source's variable-group
balance condition \(n_{tg}\le 2n/r\), give the matching upper
exponent. Equal incidence counts in the full cells alone do not
imply this variable-group bound when variable degrees vary.
The direct cloned
formula has \(q=\lfloor3nR/Q\rfloor\). In a supplied-equation branch on
\(n\) free variables, the source's nominal order uses
\(k(m_{\rm test}+2(\ell-1))R/Q\), with \(\ell-1\ge n\). Thus these
source calls have a uniform \(\kappa>0\).
\href{https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/main_full_version.tex\#L965-L1017}{Direct

Deleting zero and linearly dependent rows while retaining the same

\section{Optimal precision windows for retained root
jets}\label{optimal-precision-windows-for-retained-root-jets}

The largest test window certified by the canonical Hermite decoder,
after optimizing the sampled nodes and their truncation lengths, has
order \(M2^{7u/4}/u^2\). Using every native coefficient gives order
\(M2^u/u^2\) for the same decoder. Both assertions concern
coefficientwise recovery against independent errors in the data.

\subsection{Data and decoder}\label{data-and-decoder}

At the source root let \(a=2u\), \(L=2^a\), \(\alpha=2^{a+1}\),
\(d=M(2L-1)\), \(h=\lceil3M/u\rceil\), and \(p\ge1\). Assume
\(u\to\infty\) and \(M/u^2\to\infty\), as holds at the published root on
the sparse inputs under study. In the complete source grid
\(\{-L,\ldots,L-1\}\), take the native nodes \[
 \mathcal T_i=\{t\ne0:v_2(t)=i\},\qquad
 0\le i<u,\qquad D_i=|\mathcal T_i|=2^{a-i}.
\] The original jet at \(t\in\mathcal T_i\) has length \(k_i=h(u-i)\)
and known coefficients modulo \(2^{P_i}\), where \[
 P_i=p+8hD_i+8M(a+1).
\] The original implementation acquires the entire local polynomial at
this precision before retaining these jets (source lines 336--346). The
optimization here restricts to the stated retained-jet subprofiles, not
all information that implementation computes. These are the integral
local coordinates \(T=t+\alpha z\). The target coordinate is
\(T=\alpha w\). The coefficient ring is a discrete valuation ring with
uniformizer 2, with the field of fractions used for the interpolation
maps. The original source establishes the arithmetic, grid, degree,
integral disks, and single-value reconstruction.
\href{https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/quantitative-contraction.tex\#L173-L323}{Frozen
contraction, lines 173--323}

Choose \textbf{an arbitrary integer \(0\le q_t\le k_i\) at every native
node}. Write \(S_q=\sum_tq_t\), and omit nodes where \(q_t=0\). A
positive admissible window has \(1\le b\le S_q-d\). The encoder sends
each \(h_0(w)\in K[w]_{<b}\) to \(h_0(t/\alpha+z)\bmod z^{q_t}\). For a
local data jet, multiply in \(K[z]/z^{q_t}\), interpolate these products
with their unique degree-\(<S_q\) representative, substitute
\(T=\alpha w\), and truncate at \(w^b\). Exact data reconstruct the
anchor multiplication because the product polynomial has degree at most
\(d+b-1\). We say the window is \textbf{certified} if every independent
change of every retained data coefficient by an element of
\(2^{P_i}\mathcal O\) changes every entry of this linear operator by an
element of \(2^p\mathcal O\). For matrix-valued data the errors may also
be independent between matrix entries.
\href{https://github.com/dch-and-Elara/some-research-inspired-by-openais-eth-preprint/tree/main/rank-budget-barriers}{Matrix transport and representation
consequence}

\subsection{The exact precision
criterion}\label{the-exact-precision-criterion}

For \(q_t>0\), put \[
 C_t(T)=\prod_{s\ne t,\ q_s>0}
       \left(\frac{T-s}{t-s}\right)^{q_s},\qquad c_t=v_2(C_t(0)).
\] A degree-admissible window is certified if and only if for every
retained \(t\in\mathcal T_i\), \[
       (b+q_t-2)(a+1-i)\ \le\ P_i-p+c_t.                 \tag{P.1}
\] In particular, for a fixed profile its largest window is the lesser
of \(S_q-d\) and \[
 \min_{t:q_t>0}\left(2-q_t+
 \left\lfloor\frac{P_i-p+c_t}{a+1-i}\right\rfloor\right),
                                                               \tag{P.2}
\] provided the lesser value is positive. Otherwise the profile has no
positive certified window.

To prove sufficiency, the single-node decoder sends \(y(z)\) to \[
 C_t(\alpha w)
 \left[ y(z)/C_t(t+\alpha z)\right]_{<q_t}
     (w-t/\alpha)\pmod{w^b}.
\] The coefficients of \(C_t(\alpha w)/C_t(0)\) are integral because
\(v_2(\alpha/s)>0\) at each sampled \(s\). Likewise
\(1/C_t(t+\alpha z)\bmod z^{q_t}\) has integral coefficients and
constant one, as \(v_2(t-s)\le a\). The decoder entries therefore have
valuation at least \(c_t-(q_t-1)(a+1-i)\). The encoder entries have
valuation at least \(-(b-1)(a+1-i)\). Multiplication by \(z^j\) has
entries zero and one in the coefficient basis. These estimates give
(P.1).

For necessity change only the coefficient of \(z^{q_t-1}\) at a single
retained node and apply the perturbed decoder to \(w^{b-1}\). After
multiplication by \(z^{q_t-1}\), only the constant input at the node
survives. The constant coefficient of the resulting anchor jet per unit
of perturbation is exactly \[
      C_t(0)(-t/\alpha)^{q_t-1}(t/\alpha)^{b-1}.          \tag{P.3}
\] Its valuation is \(c_t-(b+q_t-2)(a+1-i)\). A perturbation of size
\(2^{P_i}\) tests (P.1) with equality in the entry estimate. For matrix
data one may use a single matrix unit. This also justifies (P.2).

\subsection{Native multiplicities}\label{native-multiplicities}

For the full native profile \(q_t=h(u-i)\), the source gives
\(c_t\ge-6hD_i\). Also \(q_t\le4M\), \(D_i\ge2^{a-u+1}\), and the odd
nodes alone give \(S_q-d\ge M(L+1)\). Hence \[
 b=\min\!\left(S_q-d,\ 4M+
          \left\lfloor \frac{2h\,2^{a-u+1}}{a+1}\right\rfloor\right)
\] is certified and is \(\Omega(M2^u/u^2)\). For a node in the deepest
shell \(i=u-1\), the defining product for \(C_t(0)\) has \(c_t\le0\). In
fact smaller-valuation nodes contribute zero, and other nodes in the
same shell can only decrease \(c_t\). There \(D_i=2^{u+1}\) and
\(a+1-i=u+2\). Inequality (P.1) yields \(b=O(h2^u/u+M)=O(M2^u/u^2)\).

\subsection{A profile attaining the optimized
order}\label{a-profile-attaining-the-optimized-order}

Set \(H=\lfloor4h/3\rfloor\), \(v=\lceil M/H\rceil+2\), and
\(q_t=H(v-i)_+\) for \(t\in\mathcal T_i\). For large \(u\),
\(H=4M/u+O(1)\), \(v=u/4+O(1)\), \(H\ge h\) and \(Hv\le M+3H\le hu\).
Thus \(1\le v\le u\) and \(q_t\le h(u-i)\). Summing the shell sizes
gives \[
 S_q=HL(2v-2+2^{1-v}),\qquad S_q-d\ \ge\ 2HL+M.         \tag{P.4}
\] For \(t\in\mathcal T_i\) with \(i<v\), let \(r=v-i\) and \(n=a-i\).
Comparing the nodes of valuations \(i+j\) with the nodes at distance of
valuation \(i+j\) from \(t\) gives the exact \textbf{finite} loss \[
 -c_t=H D_i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{j\min(j,r)}{2^j}
     =H D_i\!\left(6-\frac{2r+6}{2^r}
                          -\frac{r(n+2)}{2^n}\right).       \tag{P.5}
\] Indeed there are \(D_i/2^j\) points in each of those two classes. The
same-shell class carries multiplicity \(Hr\), and the class in shell
\(i+j\) carries \(H(r-j)_+\). Zero multiplicity outside the native
support can be included throughout the full grid. The series identity
uses \(\sum j^2/2^j=6\), \(\sum_{j>r}j(j-r)/2^j=(2r+6)/2^r\), and
\(r\le n\).

Since \(6H\le8h\), (P.5) leaves \[
 8hD_i+c_t\ \ge\ H(2r+6)2^{a-v}\ \ge\ 8H\,2^{a-v}.
\] With \(q_t\le4M\), (P.1) therefore holds for \[
 b_{\rm sat}=\min\!\left(S_q-d,\ 4M+
       \left\lfloor\frac{8H\,2^{a-v}}{a+1}\right\rfloor\right)
       =\Omega\!\left(\frac{M2^{7u/4}}{u^2}\right).         \tag{P.6}
\] The first term in the minimum has the lower bound (P.4). The second
has the asserted scale because \(H=\Theta(M/u)\) and \(a-v=7u/4+O(1)\).

\subsection{Upper bound for every nodewise
choice}\label{upper-bound-for-every-nodewise-choice}

Take any profile admitting a positive certified \(b\) and put
\(v=1+\max\{i:\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_i}q_t>0\}\). Define
\(m_i=D_i^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_i}q_t\) for \(i<v\), and set
\(m_i=0\) for all \(i\ge v\). At each retained \(t\in\mathcal T_i\) the
product defining \(C_t\) gives \[
 -c_t=K_t-D_i\sum_{j\ge1}\frac j{2^j}m_{i+j},\qquad
 K_t=\sum_{\substack{s\in\mathcal T_i\\s\ne t}}
                   q_s\big(v_2(t-s)-i\big).                 \tag{P.7}
\] Nodes of valuation less than \(i\) contribute zero. A higher shell
\(i+j\) contributes \(j\) times its total multiplicity to \(c_t\). All
same-shell contributions appear with the opposite sign. Condition (P.1)
gives \(-c_t\le8hD_i+8M(a+1)\), since \(b,q_t\ge1\).

Suppose \(m_i>0\), put \(n=a-i\) and \(W=D_i m_i\), and average \(K_t\)
with weights \(q_t/W\). For each \(s=1,\ldots,n\), the shell consists of
\(2^{s-1}\) residue classes modulo \(2^{i+s}\). Let \(W_C\) be the total
weight in class \(C\). Counting thresholds \(v_2(t-x)-i\ge s\) and
removing the diagonal pairs gives \[
 \sum_t q_t K_t
 =\sum_{s=1}^n\left(\sum_C W_C^2-\sum_t q_t^2\right)
 \ \ge\ 2(1-2^{-n})W^2-n\sum_tq_t^2.                     \tag{P.8}
\] Here Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\sum_C W_C^2\ge W^2/2^{s-1}\). As
\(q_t\le4M\) and \(D_i=2^n\), the average \(K_t\) is at least
\(2(D_i-1)m_i-4Mn\). Combining (P.7) and (P.8) gives \[
 2m_i-\sum_{j\ge1}\frac j{2^j}m_{i+j}
 \le 8h+\frac{8M(a+1)+4Mn+2m_i}{D_i}
 \le 8h+\frac{M(12a+16)}{2^{u+1}} .                        \tag{P.9}
\] For an empty shell the left side is nonpositive, so (P.9) holds there
too. The last estimate uses \(D_i\ge 2^{u+1}\), \(n\le a\), and
\(m_i\le4M\).

Put \(B=4h+M(12a+16)/2^{u+2}\) and \(p_j=j/2^{j+1}\) for \(j\ge1\). Then
\(m_i\le B+\sum_{j\ge1}p_jm_{i+j}\). For positive integers \(r\) set
\(f(r)=(r+2)/3\), with \(f(r)=0\) otherwise. Direct summation gives \[
 f(r)-1-\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}p_j f(r-j)=\frac{r-1}{3\,2^r}\ge0 .
\] Backward induction from \(m_i=0\) for \(i\ge v\) now gives
\(m_i\le Bf(v-i)\). Therefore \[

\textbf{Graph and terminal function.} Start from a simple cubic graph

 |\partial_G S|\ \ge\ p+\frac{\gamma}{3}(q-p)\ \ge\ \frac{\gamma}{3}q

\subsection{Relating algebraic size to a
trace}\label{relating-algebraic-size-to-a-trace}

For a reached transcript choose the set \(J_\ell\) of distinct
scalar/jet frontier values required by the certified representation. The
execution contract permits cached subcomputations, repeated reads,
arbitrary work to compute a value, and batched emissions.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  A trace consists of its actually executed charged events \(e\), with
  total positive cost \(T_\ell=\sum_e c_e\).
\item
  Each event has a record \(I_e\subseteq J_\ell\) of completed frontier
  values it delivers or individually materializes, with a proved bound
  \(|I_e|\le Wc_e\). A promised simultaneous bound for a concrete vector
  instruction can use its own \(W_e\) and sum \(\sum_e W_ec_e\).
\item
  Every index of \(J_\ell\) belongs to some \(I_e\). A value supplied by
  preprocessing shared by all inputs may enter as an ordinary fixed
  constant. Precomputed values depending on the actual signs require a
  charge or an explicit input/advice contract. The proof uses only
  \(|J_\ell|\le\sum_e |I_e|\le WT_\ell\).
\end{enumerate}

On a fixed schedule all sign-independent, graph-only feasibility checks
can be settled before the signs arrive. The rank lower bound on the
needed distinct entrances therefore gives \(T\ge2^d/(\rho W)\) when the
normalized capacity per entrance is at most \(\rho 2^{-d}\) and there is
no bypass. For the two original scalar/jet types one can instead keep
the different \(d_s\) and \(s_\lambda\) in (X.10). A sum assembled by an
arbitrary fixed linear DAG is covered because the coefficient of each
distinct entrance equals the sum of its path coefficients. Constant
batch width, polynomial batch width, and taking polynomially many
coordinates from one vector all preserve the exponential scale.

For a routed program the trace covers only its selected leaf. Combine
its coverage with the prefix-free bound (X.11) for a worst-case claim
about a single reached input. An uncharged selector depending jointly on
the signs requires the rank of its full predicate or an actual
source-scope factorization. Path bits are not a substitute for predicate
rank.

Implementations that assemble an entire symbolic answer without
producing all chosen entrance values need a different frontier or a
different trace proof. A circuit can expand exponentially many monomials
with polynomial work. A local cache or an exact fixed linear

\(s_v\) flips, while its unique quadratic monomial \(z_vx_v\) survives.
These facts invalidate unique ownership of the old unary signs in the
new list of factors.
\href{https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/main_full_version.tex\#L3363-L3436}{Tanner
checks and shortening}

There is an exact additional obstruction at the early queries. If a hash
matrix has binary row rank \(h\), choose \(h\) independent indexed rows
with the same system of zeros. The resulting test polynomials \(P_i\)
have degree at most two in \(2N\) variables. For \(h<N\),

\[
                 2^{N-h}\ \mid\ N(A).
\]

Indeed, for any Boolean quadratic \(q\), put \(W(q)=\sum_x(-1)^{q(x)}\)
and let \(B(x,y)=q(x+y)+q(x)+q(y)+q(0)\) be its bilinear polarization.
Changing variables in the square gives

\[
 W(q)^2
 =\sum_y(-1)^{q(y)+q(0)}\sum_x(-1)^{B(x,y)}
 =2^{2N}\sum_{y:\,B(\cdot,y)=0}(-1)^{q(y)+q(0)}.
\]

Thus \(2^N\) divides every integral \(W(q)\), including zero.
Orthogonality on the equation values gives
\(2^h N(A)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_2^h}W(\sum_i a_iP_i)\), proving the
divisibility. In particular, all early candidates of rank below \(N\)
have even counts for every sign word. An instance with \(F(s)=1\) needs
at least \(N\) independent rows at its correct final query, so starting
with \(<m/B=o(N)\) rows needs \(\Omega(N)\) appends. The upper bits of
the completed integer counts are available to the greedy comparisons.
This arithmetic observation supplies no exponential time bound for
implementing those choices and does not establish a separated
representation of the terminal output of the adaptive route. The divisibility statement is formalized by \nolinkurl{polynomial_hash_rank_divisible}.

\section{Consequences, computational boundaries, and open
problems}\label{consequences-computational-boundaries-and-open-problems}

\subsection{What the common invariant
resolves}\label{what-the-common-invariant-resolves}

The same tested target map governs the field degree budget, the finite
precision reconstruction, finite-algebra readouts and the final count
representation. The invariant is the rank or module length that a
certified observation channel can carry on one fixed restriction. A
change of basis, signed cancellation, a different order for fixed linear
operations, and ordinary reuse of a register leave the identity intact.
Mixed layouts change the capacity of each entrance and the geometric
problem of finding one restriction; they do not require separate output
matrices.

The source-specific theorems quantify two distinct constraints. The main
routine's exceptional measurements contain many genuinely attainable
Boolean values, and the mandated Taylor/local iterations have a matching
optimized exponent. The Appendix D precision schedule constrains how far
its retained jets can certify the specified block decoder, and this
window can be optimized sharply even over nonuniform choices within
valuation shells. Neither fact by itself forces an arbitrary algorithm
to materialize these data.

\subsection{Specific remaining mathematical
problems}\label{specific-remaining-mathematical-problems}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Native-register classification.} Determine whether the two
  cheap group products in the cross-route example can be realized by a
  polynomial number of actual MAIN jet registers and D probe values
  under their native formulas. Certified label scopes alone settle
  neither direction.
\item
  \textbf{Adaptive preprocessing.} Develop an invariant for the genuine
  coefficient functions created by Tanner checks and greedy parity rows.
  Their overlapping dependence on original signs invalidates one-owner
  arguments. Low bits of early quadratic-system counts can vanish
  identically; upper bits still inform adaptive choices.
\item
  \textbf{Implicit aggregation.} Characterize which dependencies among
  attainable measurement values permit an efficient aggregate without
  enumerating all fibers or Taylor pairs. The projection bound limits
  lossless recoding, whereas it does not prevent a sufficient statistic
  specific to the final summation.
\item
  \textbf{Decoders beyond canonical retained jets.} The precision
  optimum concerns exactly the degree representatives, native
  coefficient precisions and independent errors stated in its theorem.
  The source actually computes whole local polynomials before retaining
  the jets. Additional coefficients, correlated errors, alternative test
  spaces and rank-only robustness can evade that particular optimization
  problem.
\item
  \textbf{Full formal composition.} Formalize the observation lattice,
  native denominator arithmetic, weighted matching selection and
  correlated packing, then extract the source algorithms into the
  verified interfaces. Successful model compilation is not a substitute
  for those bridges.
\end{enumerate}

The correct next question is therefore which extra structure of native
registers excludes the exhibited cheap cross-route product, or permits
it in a useful wider family. A proof in either direction would refine
the model. Assuming that all nonlinear computation is expensive would
decide this question by definition and would not constitute a complexity
barrier.

\subsection{A witness in the window read by direct MAIN}
For the bounded-occurrence direct input in display (M.7), write \(n\) for
the variable count, \(Q\) for the number of nodes and \(R\) for the
printed interpolation width. The direct polynomial has degree \(3n-2\)
on the family under consideration. The source allocates the nominal cap
\(3n\). At cap \(d\) the orders kept at each node are \(0\le j<J(d)\),
where \(J(d)=\lfloor dR/Q\rfloor+1\).

\textbf{Window lemma.} Each high-rank coefficient witness for cap \(d\)
is a witness for every cap \(d'\ge d\) of the same recipe, with the same
cuts, node, order and matrix.

\emph{Proof.} Choose the cuts, the node \(t\), the order \(j\) and the
remaining address indices furnished by the original witness. Integer
division is monotone, so \(j<J(d)\le J(d')\). Use \(j\) itself in the
larger address set. The Hasse coefficient evaluated at this address is
computed from the same polynomial recipe and the same grid. Neither
its entries nor its flattening depend on the upper bound used to type
the index. All the balance, order and rank inequalities therefore hold
with their original witnesses. Apply this lemma with \(d=3n-2\) and
\(d'=3n\). No equality of the two lists of retained addresses is needed.
The sparse MAIN construction keeps its own grid and parity argument.
\(\square\)

\subsection{Every source collision packing on the same graph}
Let \(F\) be a finite, nonempty loopless cubic graph on \(N\) vertices.
Fix a rational \(0<\gamma\le3\) and suppose every vertex subset \(U\)
has at least \((\gamma/3)\min(|U|,N-|U|)\) boundary edges. Use the
cloned CNF, its physical clause network, the \(\ell=1\) root and the
printed parameters of Section 11. In the formal statement the source
extension polynomial is monic, has degree \(4\lambda\), and has
irreducible reduction modulo two. All labels and the packing are chosen
before the pin input. Write \(M=24N\) for the number of physical wires,
\(a\) for the full word length, \(u\) for the number of retained
coordinates, \(D=64^{2a}\) for the number of ordered word cells and
\(\Delta\) for the source degree bound, which is at least three.

\textbf{Theorem.} For all sufficiently large \(N\) (threshold depending
only on \(\gamma\)), take \emph{any} physical packing \(P\) with cell
loads \(X_c\) satisfying
\[
  \Phi(P)=\sum_c(X_c-M/D)^2\le 2M\Delta .
\]
The literal root menu of this same packing has at least
\(2^{\lfloor c_\gamma N/\sqrt{\ln N}\rfloor}\) slots for a fixed
\(c_\gamma>0\). For every sign word \(\sigma\), its literal root visit
list has at least this length. In particular this holds for every
minimizing tie rule in the printed conditional-expectation construction.

\emph{Proof of the selector guarantee.} Start with independent uniform
labels at all row and column vertices. Expanding the squared loads as
a sum over ordered wire pairs gives a contribution equal to the
probability of cell agreement minus \(1/D\) for each pair. Wires with
disjoint endpoints have independent cells and contribute zero. At most
\(2M\Delta\) ordered pairs share an endpoint, and each contributes at
most one. Thus the initial expectation is at most \(2M\Delta\).
After fixing any partial assignment the conditional score is evaluated
from the very same ordered pairs. For each side use agreement
probability one for the same endpoint, or the dot product of the two
marginal label distributions for distinct endpoints. Multiply the row
and column agreement probabilities and subtract the constant \(M^2/D\)
from the sum. This equals the conditional expectation of \(\Phi\),
since the two sides use disjoint sets of vertex variables.

At the next unfixed vertex the current score is a weighted average of
the possible next scores. Every minimizer gives a score no larger than
the current one, even when several labels tie. A minimum exists on the
nonempty finite alphabet. Fixing one fresh vertex per step produces a
complete assignment, and its conditional mean is its actual score.
Padding classes to the printed multiple of \(2^a\) retains the labels
of every original wire. Hence the bound applies to the packing that
was chosen, for every tie rule. A choice that merely keeps the
conditional mean from increasing gives the same inequality by
transitivity.

\emph{From cells to cuts.} The printed size guard is \(2D^2\Delta\le M\).
Consequently \(X_c\le M/D+\sqrt{2M\Delta}\le2M/D\) in each cell. Pick
for each original vertex the clause-side half of its unary-pin wire.
These \(N\) wires are distinct, and the row word there is exactly the
one seen by the public root. Fixing its \(u\) retained coordinates leaves
\(D/64^u\) possible full cells. For each retained word \(x\) its
empirical mass among the pins is therefore at most
\[
  N^{-1}(D/64^u)(2M/D)=48\,64^{-u}.
\]
Each one-coordinate symbol has mass at most \(48/64=3/4\).
A symbol of mass in \([1/4,3/4]\) gives a balanced cut by itself.
Otherwise accumulate the symbols of mass less than \(1/4\) until first
reaching \(1/4\). Their total is then less than \(1/2\). This gives a
quarter-balanced union of symbols at each retained coordinate.

\emph{One matching for this packing.} Here is the precise specialization
used by the Lean proof. Choose one such cut \(U\) (eventually \(u>0\))
and introduce a single auxiliary coordinate over 32 symbols that takes
only two values, according as the vertex belongs to \(U\) or to its
complement. Each of its atoms has mass at most \(3/4=24/32\). Apply
the two-layout matching theorem of Section 5 with this auxiliary
layout and the actual retained layout of \(P\). In its numerical budget
\(31104(1+u^2)\) is no greater than \(31104(Q^2+u^2)\), where \(Q\ge1\)
is the printed MAIN node count. The printed readiness inequalities
thus suffice. The auxiliary layout is used only to invoke a proved
matching theorem. It changes no address or calculation of the evaluator.

The resulting oriented induced matching and symbol cuts have endpoint
imbalance at least
\[
 d_D=\left\lfloor b_{\gamma/3} N/(\lfloor\sqrt u\rfloor+1)\right\rfloor
\]
at every retained coordinate of \(P\), where \(b_{\gamma/3}>0\) is the
matching coefficient of Section 5. Write \(m_*\) for the size of the
matching. Its OR matrix has rank \(2^{m_*}\) modulo two. The integral
lift in Section 11 has the same full rank over the coefficient field,
whereas each scheduled product on this very packing has rank at most
\(2^{m_*-d_D}\). Subadditivity implies \(2^{d_D}\le|\mathcal I_D(P)|\).
The printed depth estimate gives
\(d_D\ge\lfloor c_\gamma N/\sqrt{\ln N}\rfloor\).
Finally the ledger visits each member of \(\mathcal I_D(P)\) on
\emph{every} pin input, so the same lower bound holds for its length.
The size threshold precedes all choices of graph, packing and input.
The fixed-density bit-time upper bound in Section 11 is quoted from
the source evaluator. The quantity counted in this proof is the
number of literal root visits. \(\square\)
